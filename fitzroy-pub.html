For a pub, Google Maps does more work than the website

Most people picking a pub are on their phone, and most never type in a website address. They search “pub near me” or “pubs Fitzroy” and choose from the map: photos, stars, open now, a book button. So the Google listing gets set up first. The website's job is to back it up with a menu Google can read, a booking button on every page and a What's On that's actually current.

Your address comes with a past

The building has been a pub since the mid-1800s and has had plenty of names: the Brunswick Hotel, the Old Colonial Inn, then 127 Brunswick and Hotel Fitzroy, with Ichi Ni Nana, Village People and the Upside rooftop inside. Broadsheet still lists it as Hotel Fitzroy, permanently closed.

That's a mess to clean up, and it's also a head start. People who remember the OC still search for it, and Fitzroy's history sites already write about the building.

Google first

Google can show the pub from December, three months before you open.

Google lets a new business enter its opening date in advance. Once the listing is verified, it appears on Google 90 days before that date, and you can post fit-out photos and opening news to it. For a 1 March opening, that's 1 December, which is why the SEO starts then. After opening, Google tags you “Recently opened” for another 90 days.

What we set up

The listing as a pub at the right address, with your opening month and a link to the coming-soon page. Google picks how to verify a business, and these days it's usually a short video of the venue, the signage and proof you run it, so we time that with the signs going up. Then the first fit-out photos and an opening post. On opening day it switches over to hours, menu, the booking link and the details people filter by.

Clearing out the old listings

We'll find every listing at 125–129 Brunswick Street (Hotel Fitzroy, Ichi Ni Nana, Upside, Village People) and make sure each one shows as closed and none gets merged into yours. We won't try to keep their reviews. They're about an izakaya and a rooftop bar, not your pub.

And if the old owners will hand over the old venues' web addresses, we'll point them at your site so whatever they earned isn't wasted.

Three gaps worth taking

Menus. Three of the five don't have a food menu with prices that Google can read on their own site. Google can only match a search for “parma Fitzroy” to a menu it can read.

What's On. Only Builders Arms has current, dated events. The Standard's gig guide stops in January 2024, and the Marquis has none. A What's On that's kept current feeds the Google listing every week.

Saying where they are. Three of the five don't put Fitzroy in their homepage title, and none of the five tells Google, in the code behind the page, that it's a pub with a menu, opening hours and events. Yours will do both from day one.

What none of this beats

These pubs have years of reviews and regulars behind them. A good website doesn't outrank that in a month, and we won't pretend it does. What it does is make sure that when someone finds you, on the map or in a list of new openings, there's nothing to put them off and an obvious way to book.

Who uses what. Two of the five book through SevenRooms, two through OpenTable, one through TableAgent. They're built on Squarespace (two), Weebly, WordPress and Webflow.
