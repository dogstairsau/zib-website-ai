In plain English · digital labels

Digital labels: name tags on the blueprint.

One idea this audit keeps coming back to, explained in builder's terms.

Imagine you built a hospital for Google.

To Google, your finished building is just a shape in the dark. It can guess what's inside, guess slowly, or hand the answer to a competitor. A digital label is the spec sticker on the plans: “commercial builder · healthcare · Victoria · Royal Melbourne Hospital · completed.” Now Google knows exactly what it's looking at, and so do ChatGPT and Google's AI answers.

Without them: Google guesses from the page text, and AI tools skip Alchemy entirely.

With them: you show up in Google's map results and richer listings, and AI tools name you directly.

Alchemy today: the labels that say “we're a commercial builder, here's where we work, here's what we've delivered” are missing on every page.
Current state · AI answers

Never mentioned in AI answers.

AI tools don't name Alchemy, and the missing labels explain why. The upside: competitors are just as absent, so this is open ground.

Being named by AI tools
  • Mentioned in AI answers: zero. Nothing across Google's AI answers, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot or Grok.
  • Google and AI can't tell what you are. The labels that identify Alchemy as a Victorian commercial builder aren't on the site for them to read.
Winning the question-and-answer box
  • Nothing to quote. There's no question-and-answer content, so Google and AI have nothing to pull when someone asks a question.
  • The raw material is already there. Articles like “The Unseen Risk in Healthcare Buildings” are useful, they just need structuring as clear questions and answers.

AI can't name a business it can't understand. Add the labels and the question-and-answer content, and Alchemy gets named before competitors move.
