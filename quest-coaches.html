What's wrong

Your website isn't broken. It was never built to win work.

Every page loads, nothing is missing, and Google hasn't punished you. What you have is a brochure from about ten years ago that says you own buses. It doesn't answer what a school, a business or a club asks before booking, so you've simply been out-built.

Someone on their phone can't call you

Normally a phone turns a number on a website into a button you tap to call. Your site specifically tells it not to, and there's no tap-to-call link to make up for it. So the 1300 number sits there as plain text — to ring you, someone has to memorise it, leave your site and dial manually. Your site was also never updated for phones, so it loads shrunken and that number is half the size it should be.

Crown and Ventura both have two tappable numbers on every page. This is the cheapest fix on the list and the one costing you the most.

And you can't see where your work comes from

Your visitor tracking stopped collecting anything in 2023, and quotes happen over on your separate booking system. So nobody can tell you which suburb, service or search brought last month's bookings in. That gets fixed in the first fortnight, because everything after it is measured against it.

Two smaller things worth knowing: one of your pages is titled “Crown Bus Charter”, a competitor's name, which is why you turn up when people search for Crown. And there's still a COVID notice on every page with a footer that says 2021.

How the page count actually grows

Eleven pages go live with the build. The other 47 are content, and the monthly work writes two to three of them a month. That's the honest rate — each page needs its own research, words, photos and questions, which is exactly why they're not in the launch.

The order matters more than the pace. Schools and corporate get written first, because that's where the demand and the repeat bookings are. If you ever want it faster, the lever is more copywriting in the monthly work, not a bigger build.

How the pages get written

Three layouts get designed once — trip pages, area pages, bus pages — then each page is filled with its own words, photos, questions and reviews. The layout repeats, the words never do. Google is good now at spotting pages that are the same thing with the names swapped, and it buries them.

The nine area pages are deliberately last. They matter — your own lost searches show demand in Pakenham, Shepparton and Traralgon — but they only work once the service pages above them are earning. And we won't build a page per suburb: nine areas, each with real local detail, and more only where the searches show up.
