The pattern

A hedge trim and an emergency removal share almost no economics.

Tree work has one of the widest job-value ranges of any trade. Most operators market for volume and absorb the qualification cost in quoting time. The better play is filtering at the ad, not at the driveway.

  • Unqualified enquiries cost you a truck and half a day. Every quote is a site visit. A campaign that generates fifty enquiries at the wrong job value is worse than one that generates fifteen at the right one.
  • Storm and season demand arrives all at once. Post-storm and pre-summer peaks are where the year is made, and they need spend flexed hard rather than held flat.
  • Council permits and protected species stop the job after you've won it. Content addressing permit requirements by council area qualifies buyers early, ranks for a genuinely searched question, and positions you as the operator who knows the rules.
Why generic marketing fails here

Cheap leads are the expensive ones.

A generic agency optimises for cost per lead, which in this vertical actively rewards the cheapest, least valuable enquiries. The metric that matters is cost per quoted job above your value threshold.

Getting there means qualification built into the ad copy and the form — job type, tree height, access — plus spend that surges when storms and seasons move demand. Fewer enquiries, better ones, and a crew that quotes jobs worth winning.
