The pattern

The buyer already knows what they need. They land on whoever's site contains it.

The person sourcing a component, a piece of equipment or a bulk supply already knows what they need. They search by specification, part number, capacity or material — and they land on whoever's site actually contains those terms. Most manufacturer websites talk about heritage and quality commitment, and rank for neither.

  • Your catalogue is a PDF, so it's invisible. Product data locked in a downloadable PDF or a Flash-era table cannot rank. Specification content rendered as indexable pages is the single biggest untapped asset in industrial search.
  • Long buying cycles, and no visibility into the first two-thirds. Industrial purchases involve months of research and multiple stakeholders before anyone makes contact. If the only conversion action is "request a quote", you're invisible for most of the journey.
  • You're competing with distributors for your own products. Resellers often outrank manufacturers on their own product terms because they publish better structured content.
Why generic marketing fails here

Unglamorous, technical, and it works.

Consumer SEO tactics don't transfer. Industrial search rewards technical depth: specification pages, application-based content, comparison tables, downloadable data republished as indexable HTML.

It's unglamorous work most agencies won't do, and it compounds for years — because a specification page that ranks keeps ranking long after a paid campaign stops.

Specification architecture

The next frontier · AEO & GEO

Assistants are already doing technical sourcing. PDFs don't get cited.

AI assistants are heavily used for technical sourcing — "supplier of a component with this specification in Australia". Structured specification data is exactly what gets extracted. Manufacturers with indexable technical content are already being cited; those with PDFs are not.
