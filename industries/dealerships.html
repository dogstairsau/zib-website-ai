The pattern

You pay to represent a brand that outranks you for your own stock.

A franchised dealer's search reality is upside down: the carline site has more authority, more budget and a different objective. It wants the model sold anywhere. You need the buyer in your showroom — and the searches that decide that are local, specific and entirely winnable.

  • A multi-site group is not one local market. Every showroom competes in its own suburb, with its own map pack, its own reviews and its own service catchment. Groups that run one website and one generic profile hand each local market to the single-site dealer down the road.
  • The year is spiky and the budget is flat. Plate change, EOFY and runout compress enormous demand into short windows. If the surge campaigns aren't already built — audiences, creative, budget held back to fund them — the window closes while the agency is still briefing.
  • Fixed operations could fund the whole programme and it's unmarketed. Service, parts and tyres often out-earn new car sales on margin, the searches are barely contested, and almost every dealer's marketing ignores them completely.
Why generic marketing fails here

Inventory feeds and co-op creative don't choose a dealer.

The standard dealer stack — an inventory-feed platform plus the OEM's co-op campaigns — sells the model. Nothing in it decides which dealership the buyer walks into. That decision is made on local search, the map pack and reviews, and it's the layer almost nobody manages deliberately.

Our work sits exactly there: per-showroom local presence, surge campaigns timed to the sales calendar, and a fixed-operations line that compounds while the sales floor rides the cycle.
