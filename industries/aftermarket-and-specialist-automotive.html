The pattern

The buyer knows their vehicle. They search it, the product and the problem.

Aftermarket and specialist buyers arrive with the vehicle already in the query — the model, the upgrade, the job it has to do. The businesses that win are the ones whose sites are organised the way those searches are asked, and whose campaigns respect that a big build is decided over weeks, not clicks.

  • Fitment search is specific and your site is organised by brand. Buyers search vehicle-first — the model, then the product, then the spec. A catalogue organised by product brand misses the query entirely. Vehicle and application architecture is the single biggest ranking asset in the category.
  • High-ticket builds are researched for weeks and bought once. A full touring setup or suspension package is a considered, four-or-five-figure decision. Search captures the moment of intent; the weeks before it belong to remarketing and consideration creative, and most operators run neither.
  • The workshop is a local business even when the store ships nationally. Fitting bays, panel work and installs are won on local intent and Google Business Profile — a different game to the national ecommerce side, and it deserves its own campaign.
Why generic marketing fails here

Not generic ecommerce. Not dealership marketing either.

A generic ecommerce agency optimises the cart and ignores the vehicle. A generic automotive agency sells inventory feeds built for dealers. Specialist aftermarket sits between them: fitment content that ranks, high-intent paid capture, and a consideration layer for the builds that take weeks to decide.

Run together, the economics are exceptional — one Zib client's Google Ads account delivers leads at under ten dollars each in a category where the product sells for thousands.

The next frontier · AEO & GEO

"What's the best upgrade for my model?" Assistants answer with whoever published the fitment.

"Best suspension upgrade for a specific model", "will this fit my vehicle", "legal lift height in my state" — these are assistant queries now, and the answers are extracted from structured fitment and compliance content. Brochure categories don't get cited; application data does.

Answer Engine Optimisation

Fitment data an assistant can extract.

  • Product, offer and FAQ schema across the catalogue
  • State-specific compliance content — lift heights, engineering, ADR — kept current

The specialist the answer names.
