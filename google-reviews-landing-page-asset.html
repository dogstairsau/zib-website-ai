Your Google reviews are your best landing page asset.

Everything on your landing page is a claim, except your reviews. Here is how to put them to work so more of the clicks you pay for turn into enquiries.

MG Michael Glasser 7 October 2026 6 min read
Proof beats promises.

Most businesses spend weeks on the headline, the hero image and the offer on a landing page, then tuck their reviews into a slider at the bottom, if they appear at all. That is backwards. The person landing from an ad has never heard of you. Everything you say about yourself on that page is a claim. Your reviews are the only thing on it that someone else said.

If you are paying for every click, reviews are the cheapest conversion lift available to you. You already own them. They cost nothing to add. And they answer the one question every cold visitor is quietly asking: can I trust these people with my money?

Why reviews do the heavy lifting

A landing page has a few seconds to earn trust from a stranger. Copy can explain what you do. Photos can show it. Neither proves you do it well. A review from a real customer, with a name attached, does that in one sentence.

It also changes the comparison the visitor is making. Someone searching for a service usually opens three or four results in tabs. They are not reading every word. They are scanning for reasons to rule businesses out. A visible rating and a handful of specific, recent reviews give them a reason to keep yours open while they close the others.

Your ad gets the click. Your reviews decide whether the click turns into a lead.

Where reviews belong on the page

Placement matters more than volume. Ten reviews at the bottom of a long page will be seen by a fraction of the people who see one line of proof at the top.

Above the fold, next to the headline

Put your star rating and review count where the eye lands first: in the header, or directly above or below the main headline. One line is enough. The rating, the number of reviews and where they come from, such as Google. It sets the tone before the visitor reads anything else.

Beside the form or call button

The moment someone hesitates is the moment they are about to hand over their details. A short review placed next to the enquiry form or call button meets that hesitation head on. Pick one that speaks to the worry people have at that step: responsiveness, honest pricing, turning up when they said they would.

A dedicated section further down

For visitors who scroll, give reviews their own section with four to six full quotes. This is where the detail does its work. Longer reviews that describe a specific job, a problem solved or a smooth experience persuade the reader who is still weighing it up.

Choose reviews that match the page

A generic "great service, highly recommend" review is better than nothing, but not by much. The reviews that convert are the ones that match what the visitor came for.

  • If the page is about one service, lead with reviews that mention that service by name.
  • If your buyers worry about cost, show a review that talks about clear or fair pricing.
  • If your buyers worry about disruption, show one about a tidy site, a quick turnaround or good communication.
  • If you sell to businesses as well as consumers, include one from each, so both see themselves on the page.

This is the same principle as matching your ad copy to your landing page. The closer the proof is to the visitor's situation, the harder it works. It is one of the reasons we build separate landing pages for separate services rather than sending every ad to the homepage.

Show them properly

How you present reviews affects how much people believe them.

  • Quote them word for word. Do not tidy up the grammar or trim a sentence into a stronger claim. Real reviews read like real people, and visitors can tell the difference.
  • Include a name and source. "Sarah, Google review" is far more credible than an anonymous quote.
  • Show the real rating and count. A 4.8 from 150 reviews is more persuasive than a 5.0 from three, and both are more persuasive than a rating with no number beside it. Keep the figures current.
  • Use the recognisable marks. Star ratings and the logo of the review platform give instant recognition. A visitor knows what a Google rating means without reading a word.
  • Skip heavy widgets. Third-party review widgets can slow a page down, and slow pages lose paid traffic. On a landing page, a few hand-picked reviews written into the page usually beat a live feed.

One more thing: never edit, invent or selectively present reviews in a way that gives a false impression. Beyond the obvious trust problem, Australian consumer law treats misleading reviews seriously. Real reviews, shown honestly, are all you need.

Your reviews start before the landing page

The work does not start on the page. It starts with how you ask for reviews in the first place.

  1. Ask every happy customer, every time. Make it part of how a job closes, not something you remember once a quarter. A short message with a direct link works best.
  2. Ask them to mention the service. A simple prompt like "it would help if you mention what we did for you" produces reviews you can actually use on service pages.
  3. Reply to every review. A thoughtful reply shows prospects that you care about the experience, and it gives the reviewer a reason to remember you.
  4. Connect your Google Business Profile to your ads. Location assets can show your address and rating alongside your Google Ads, so the proof starts working before anyone even clicks.

If you are running ads to a page that hides its reviews, you are paying full price for clicks and then asking strangers to take your word for it. Move your rating to the top, put a matching review beside the form, and give your best reviews a section of their own. It is one of the fastest, cheapest changes you can make, and it usually pays for itself in the first month of spend.
