Executive summary

Concentration, not decline.

Project Better appears for far fewer searches than a year ago, but the terms it retains are stronger and estimated traffic has held steady. The risk is not the ranking — it is that discovery now runs through a very narrow group of pages, and several membership journeys are broken.

The three highest-leverage actions
  1. Repair the membership journeys. Four site addresses and two member portal pages return errors, including Fitness Passport, the membership portal and the member password reset. Every one of these interrupts somebody already deciding to join or trying to get back in.
  2. Turn the pre-launch location pages into real pages. Brunswick, Prahran, Kensington and Abbotsford carry only a "coming" line and an email form. Brunswick in particular now competes with the open Brunswick East gym for the same suburb signals.
  3. Widen discovery beyond the homepage. The homepage carries roughly 77% of estimated traffic. Stronger location, membership, amenity and personal-training pages move people closer to the next step instead of routing nearly everyone through the front door.
Scope note

This is an overview audit built on Ahrefs data plus live verification of specific addresses. No full technical crawl and no Google Business Profile review have been carried out — both are set out as open items in the scope section, and the local-pack work is likely to be the next significant lever.

1.3 — Branded versus non-branded

The narrower keyword set is capturing genuine new demand

Non-branded traffic grew over the period while branded traffic softened. That is the clearest available evidence that the remaining keywords are pulling in people who did not already know the brand, rather than simply catching those searching for project better by name.

It is also why the headline "down 81% on keywords" should not be read as a collapse. A large share of what disappeared was thin, low-intent coverage. What remains converts search demand into gym visits.

Part 2 — What changed

The move to structured location addresses.

The website moved to a structured set of location addresses. Most of that work was done properly. A small number of gaps remain, and each one sits on a membership journey.

2.1 — What was handled correctly

Most old addresses redirect to their current equivalent

Brunswick East, West Melbourne, East Brunswick Village, Collingwood, memberships, contact, about, FAQs, personal training and privacy all redirect properly. That preserved a good deal of search visibility. No further repair is needed on any of these.

2.2 — The gaps that remain

Four addresses on the site return errors

All were verified live rather than read from cached crawl data. Each one interrupts somebody who may already be considering eligibility, joining, or getting into their existing membership.

Fix the address, not the link

A Project Better help article still contains a link to the broken membership portal address. Redirecting the underlying address resolves the help-article link and every other reference to it at once — which is faster and safer than hunting individual links.

2.3 — The member portal subdomain

Members hitting error pages when they try to get back in

The member portal subdomain is visible in search and ranks for brand terms, yet several of its addresses return errors — including the password reset page and the main member dashboard, both confirmed live.

A member trying to reset a password and landing on an error page is a retention issue before it is a search issue. Alongside fixing the errors, portal and login addresses should be kept out of search results entirely — they are not pages a prospective member should ever land on from Google.

2.4 — Duplicate and low-value addresses

Two Collingwood pages, and some housekeeping

There are two live Collingwood pages presenting the same gym and the same address. This splits search signals and makes it harder for Google to choose the best page. Alongside them sit several addresses that add nothing for customers.

2.5 — Where visibility softened

Four themes weakened over the period

This does not prove the site changes caused every movement. Rankings move for many reasons. What it does show is exactly where stronger pages and clearer internal links could recapture relevant demand — and three of the four map directly onto a gap identified above.

Part 4 — Growth opportunities and risks

Local discovery is the near-term opportunity.

The keyword set is dominated by "near me" style searches across multiple physical sites. That makes location pages, business profiles and consistent local detail the highest-value work available.

4.1 — The pre-launch location pages

Four pages carrying a date and an email form

Brunswick, Prahran, Kensington and Abbotsford are currently pre-launch pages carrying only "coming 2026" or "coming 2028" messaging and an email notification form. They need accurate location details, a clear opening proposition, reasons to choose Project Better, and a tracked call to action for presale membership or trial interest.

The Brunswick conflict

The thin Brunswick page now sits alongside the actual Brunswick East gym and competes for the same suburb signals. That is part of why Brunswick terms weakened. The Brunswick page needs to be built so it supports Brunswick East rather than competing with it — clear, distinct positioning and internal links that point demand to the gym that is actually open.

4.2 — Supporting content

There is currently nowhere to put it

The blog address returns an error, so the site has no working home for supporting content. Restoring it is a prerequisite, not an optional extra.

Useful topics should answer real membership questions and reinforce each gym's location, facilities, personal training, Fitness Passport access and free-trial offer. The goal is not volume. It is helping more nearby customers find the right gym and take the next step — and avoiding the trap of treating close keyword variations as separate audiences.

Observed

Promotional links, mostly nofollow

Many of the unusual links are marked "nofollow", which generally tells search engines not to treat them as endorsements, and a large share carry promotional anchor text advertising third-party SEO services.

Interpretation

Monitor, do not panic

This pattern should be reviewed and monitored. It is not evidence of a penalty, and not evidence of deliberate manipulation by Project Better. Passive accumulation of this kind is common and frequently harmless.

Explained

The Brunswick Zebras sponsorship

A local sponsorship creates many repeated links from one website. That is one genuine relationship, not hundreds of separate endorsements, and is not a cause for alarm on its own.

4.4 — Competitors and paid search

Room to grow coverage, and an untested channel

Competitors including KĀYA, Chasing Better, East Brunswick Village and Cheeky Winx currently appear for a broader range of searches. Their estimated traffic is not directly comparable, because the brands, services and locations differ. The useful takeaway is simply that Project Better has room to grow its coverage of high-intent local and service searches.

Ahrefs detected no paid-search activity. That may offer an additional route to market once launch timing, budgets and conversion tracking are confirmed — particularly for high-intent membership and free-trial searches, where organic position alone caps how much of the demand can be captured.

Part 5 — Recommended priorities

In order, and for a reason.

Repairs first, because they protect demand that already exists. Then presale locations, then consolidation, then coverage, then measurement.

Priority 01

Repair the membership journeys

These are people who have already decided to act. Every day the errors stay live, that intent is wasted.

  • Send the old Fitness Passport address to the current Fitness Passport page
  • Redirect the old blog, contact-us and membership portal addresses to their live equivalents, so every existing reference resolves
  • Fix the error pages on the member portal subdomain, starting with password reset and the member dashboard
  • Keep portal and login addresses out of search results
Priority 02

Strengthen Brunswick and Prahran for presale demand

Two suburbs where demand exists now and the page cannot yet capture it.

  • Add approved location details, distinctive local benefits, and tracked membership or free-trial calls to action
  • Make sure the Brunswick page supports rather than competes with Brunswick East
  • Confirm Kensington's status before further promotion — it already ranks position 5 while not open
  • Treat Abbotsford as a longer-horizon placeholder
Priority 03

Create one clear Collingwood destination

Two pages for one gym forces Google to choose. Make the choice for it.

  • Combine the best information into a single page
  • Redirect the duplicate address to it
  • Remove the low-value background addresses — the old "page not found" address, the unused location-combine page, and the automatic archive and feed addresses
Priority 04

Expand high-intent search coverage

Protect what is working while widening the base beyond a single page.

  • Protect the homepage and personal-training gains
  • Build useful location, membership, facility, Fitness Passport and free-trial content
  • Restore a working home for that content
  • Avoid treating close keyword variations as separate audiences
Priority 05

Measure and scale what produces enquiries

Ahrefs estimates start the conversation. Search Console, analytics and lead data finish it.

  • Track search clicks, form completions, trials and memberships
  • Monitor the unusual links without overreacting
  • Then assess a controlled paid-search test, where location readiness and lead tracking support it

Why this matters

From discovery to enquiry, free trial and membership.

Project Better still captures meaningful demand. But discovery runs through a narrow group of searches and pages, and several of the paths from that discovery to a membership are broken.

The opportunity

Rebuild breadth without disturbing what works

The concentrated keyword set is performing. Widening local visibility is additive work — it does not require touching the homepage and personal-training gains that are already delivering.

The cost of waiting

Six broken journeys, live right now

Every one of them sits in front of somebody who has already decided to check eligibility, join, or log back in. This is demand that has already been paid for.

This work connects more local gym searches to the right Project Better offer, removes avoidable barriers in the joining and member-access journeys, and creates a clearer path from discovery to enquiry, free trial and membership.

Next steps

Start with the four 404s.

They are the cheapest fix in this document and the only ones sitting directly on a membership journey. Everything else in Part 5 can follow on a normal schedule.

  1. Approve the redirect map. Fitness Passport, blog, contact-us and membership portal — four addresses, resolved to their live equivalents.
  2. Get the member portal errors in front of whoever owns that system. Password reset and the member dashboard first.
  3. Confirm launch dates and approved detail for Brunswick, Prahran and Kensington so the location pages can be built with accurate information.
  4. Grant access to Search Console, analytics and lead tracking so the commercial return on this work can be measured rather than estimated.
Scope and limitations

What this report can and cannot tell you.

Two areas are outside what has been examined and should be treated as open.

A.1 Open area — no full technical crawl has been run

Page titles, meta descriptions, headings, internal link depth, structured data and page-speed metrics have not been assessed. The technical findings in this report come from Ahrefs' crawl sample plus live verification of specific addresses.

This matters because those are exactly the areas where a site with 84 ranking terms and one dominant page usually has recoverable ground. A full crawl is the first piece of work in workstream 02.

A.2 Open area — no Google Business Profile or local-pack review

No business profile or local-pack review has been carried out. Given that the keyword set is dominated by "near me" style searches across multiple physical sites, business profile optimisation, review activity and consistent location details are likely to be a significant lever — and should be the next area of work.

For a multi-site gym business, the local pack frequently sits above the organic results entirely. A strong organic position on brunswick gyms is worth considerably less if the business profile beneath it is incomplete.

A.3 Ahrefs figures are estimates, not measured sessions

Ahrefs traffic and traffic-value figures are modelled estimates. They are not actual website sessions, enquiries, memberships or revenue.

Directional conclusions — the shape of the year, the scale of the keyword narrowing, the direction of the position movements — are reliable. Absolute numbers are not. Search Console, website analytics and lead data should be used to measure the commercial return from this work.

Two specific cautions carried from the body of the report:

  • The monthly-average and single-day snapshot measures will not tie exactly, and are used for different purposes
  • August 2026 is a partial month in every figure it appears in, and is not a completed comparison
A.4 Correlation is not causation on the ranking movements

The report notes that visibility softened around brunswick gym, Fitness Passport questions, sauna collingwood and personal trainer brunswick over the same period as the address restructure.

This does not prove the site changes caused every movement. Rankings move for many reasons, including competitor activity and algorithm updates. What the overlap does establish is where stronger pages and clearer internal links would recapture relevant demand — which is actionable regardless of the original cause.
